About

Sep 2026

I am an Assistant Professor at Duke University School of Medicine, where I direct the Duke Medical Imaging & AI Jiashuo Lab. We build multimodal foundation models for clinical imaging — models pretrained jointly on volumetric images and the text radiologists actually write — and we hold them to the standard a reading room would: findings that point back to voxels, performance that survives a change of scanner, and failure modes we can name before deployment.

My earlier work was on learning without enough labels and on connecting language to pixels: uncertainty-guided co-training for semi-supervised segmentation (CVPR 2022), novel-object captioning and referring-expression grounding (ICCV 2023, ACM MM 2023), 3D instance segmentation from click-level annotations, and in-context semi-supervised learning. Clinical imaging is where those problems stop being benchmarks: annotation is expensive because it costs radiologist hours, the language is a report rather than a caption, and a wrong answer has a patient attached to it.

Most of our projects start from a question a clinical collaborator brought to us and end with an external validation cohort rather than a leaderboard. The lab opened in September 2026 and is recruiting its first members.

I read every email from prospective trainees. I am slower to answer generic ones — see Join the lab for what to send.

Research

Three directions
Direction 01 · Pretraining

Image–report pretraining that respects how studies are actually structured

A chest CT is not a 2D image and a report is not a caption. We study pretraining objectives that use what DICOM already encodes — series, phase, slice spacing, acquisition parameters, prior studies — as free supervision, and that keep 3D context instead of collapsing volumes into slices.

Methods
Contrastive and masked-modeling objectives over volumes, series-aware encoders, long-context report modeling, parameter-efficient adaptation (LoRA / adapters)
Data
De-identified CT / MR / PET / CXR with paired reports, under IRB; DICOM → NIfTI pipelines that keep provenance
Direction 02 · Grounding

Reports a radiologist can check: grounding, measurement, and uncertainty

Generated text is only useful if each finding can be traced to the voxels that produced it and to a number someone can verify. We work on phrase grounding in 3D, measurable findings (lesion diameter, volume, Hounsfield-unit ranges, SUV), calibrated uncertainty, and systematic auditing of hallucinated findings and quietly missed ones.

Metrics
Grounded report accuracy, per-finding sensitivity at fixed specificity, Dice/DSC for localized findings, calibration error, clinically-weighted error counts
Design
Reader studies with the clinicians who would use the output, not proxy annotators
Direction 03 · Validation

Generalization, subgroups, and what happens after deployment

Most imaging models degrade on the first scanner they were not trained on. We characterize that degradation across sites, vendors, protocols, and patient subgroups; develop label-efficient adaptation for new sites; and build monitoring that flags distribution drift in a live PACS setting before accuracy is lost.

Reporting
AUROC / AUPRC with 95% CI, external cohorts named explicitly, subgroup tables, decision-curve analysis
Translation
Prospective silent trials; regulatory framing (SaMD, FDA 510(k)) with Duke collaborators
